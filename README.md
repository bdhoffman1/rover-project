#  Interdisciplinary Engineering Rover Project

CAD assembly optimization, structural component redesign, and Design for Manufacturing (DFM) for a multi-disciplinary student organization rover. This project highlights collaborative engineering, SolidWorks assembly management, and manufacturing-driven design.

---

##  Key Technical Highlights

* **SolidWorks Assembly Management:** Inherited active assembly models mid-project, modifying component geometry and updating constraints to resolve mechanical conflicts.
* **Design for Manufacturing (DFM):** Redesigned structural components to simplify 3D printing parameters, cut print time, and lower production overhead.
* **Assembly Streamlining:** Optimized chassis and mounting interfaces to streamline physical assembly steps and improve serviceability.
* **Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** Worked closely alongside electrical and assembly team members to ensure mechanical updates preserved critical internal clearances.

---

##  CAD Optimization & Assembly Workflows (SolidWorks)

Working on an active multi-member assembly required systematic CAD management and geometric refinement:

* **Assembly Restructuring:** Updated mate constraints and fixed outdated part geometry to ensure seamless sub-assembly integration across the rover frame.
* **Packaging & Clearances:** Adjusted internal mounting interfaces to provide adequate clearance for electronics, wiring harnesses, and battery units.
* **Collaborative Updates:** Managed revision updates with teammates to prevent cross-component interference during rapid design iterations.

##  Manufacturing-Driven Redesign & 3D Printing

To transition the rover from digital CAD to a physical prototype efficiently, key structural components were redesigned around additive manufacturing constraints:

1. **Print Optimization:** Oriented geometry to minimize support material requirements and reduce build failures during 3D printing runs.
2. **Fastener & Mount Access:** Repositioned hardware mounting cutouts to allow standard hand tool access during physical fabrication and teardown.
3. **Weight & Material Savings:** Removed unneeded bulk from non-load-bearing structural parts without sacrificing frame rigidity.
